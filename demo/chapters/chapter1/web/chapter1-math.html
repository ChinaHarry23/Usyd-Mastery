DEMO101 — Building a Study Hub

Foundations: Counting the Pack

Three small pieces of arithmetic keep a content pack healthy: a word budget, an anchor set that must stay injective, and a path rule that decides how a relative link is written.

Counting a page

Before you judge whether a page is balanced, measure it. Two numbers are enough: the total weight and the spread between sections.

Write \(w_i\) for the word count of section \(i\) on a page with \(n\) sections. The total weight of the page and its mean section weight are

\[ W = \sum_{i=1}^{n} w_i, \qquad \bar{w} = \frac{W}{n}. \]

A page is balanced when no section carries more than twice the mean and none carries less than a third of it. Formally, for every \(i\),

\[ \tfrac{1}{3}\bar{w} \;\le\; w_i \;\le\; 2\bar{w}. \]

The lower bound matters as much as the upper one. A section that is far shorter than its neighbours is usually a heading that never earned its place in the sidebar: it takes a click, it adds a progress unit, and it says almost nothing. The upper bound catches the opposite fault, where one section quietly became the whole chapter and the remaining sections became an appendix to it.

Balance rule

A section whose weight falls outside the band \([\bar{w}/3,\, 2\bar{w}]\) is either a stub to merge upwards or a lecture to split in two.

A reading budget

Widgets cost time even though they cost no words. A budget that ignores them will always run long.

Readers move through prose at roughly \(200\) words per minute and through an interactive widget at roughly \(45\) seconds each, because a widget invites a second attempt before it invites a conclusion. For a page with \(W\) words and \(k\) widgets, the expected time in minutes is

\[ T(W, k) \;=\; \frac{W}{200} \;+\; \frac{3k}{4}. \]

Invert the formula to get the authoring constraint. If a study page should take at most \(15\) minutes, then

\[ \frac{W}{200} + \frac{3k}{4} \le 15 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad W \;\le\; 200\left(15 - \tfrac{3k}{4}\right) = 3000 - 150k. \]

So a page with two widgets has room for about \(2700\) words, and a page with six widgets has room for about \(2100\). The exchange rate is blunt but honest: every widget you add buys about \(150\) words of prose that a reader will not have time to read.

The formula is a tie-breaker, not a law

When two drafts of a section are equally good, the shorter one wins, and the budget tells you how much shorter. It is not a reason to delete the sentence that makes an idea click.

Anchors as pairs

An anchor is not a string. It is a string tied to a file, and the pair is what has to exist.

Let \(F\) be the set of pages in a pack and, for each page \(f \in F\), let \(\mathrm{ids}(f)\) be the set of id attributes written inside it. The anchor set of the pack is the set of pairs

\[ A \;=\; \bigl\{\, (f, a) \;:\; f \in F,\; a \in \mathrm{ids}(f) \,\bigr\}. \]

A reference in the pack is a written link such as ../chapters/01/study.html#sec-pack. Reading a reference produces a pair, and validation is the requirement that every reference lands inside \(A\):

\[ \forall\, r \in R: \quad \mathrm{read}(r) \in A. \]

The injective part of the story lives inside a single page. If a page has \(m\) sections, the generator expects \(m\) distinct section ids, so the map from sections to ids must be one-to-one:

\[ \bigl|\{\, \mathrm{id}(s) : s \in \text{sections}(f) \,\}\bigr| \;=\; m. \]

When that equality fails, the second section to claim an id is simply unreachable. Browsers do not complain, the page still renders, and only the link from the mind map quietly lands on the wrong paragraph. This is the single most common structural defect in a hand-written pack, and it is why ids should be read as a list rather than skimmed one at a time.

Anchor completeness

A pack is anchor-complete when every reference resolves to a pair in \(A\). Anchor completeness is checkable without rendering anything: parse the files, collect the ids, and compare the two sets.

Resolving relative paths

Every link in a pack is written relative to the file that contains it. One depth rule generates all of them.

Measure depth from the pack root, so that data/kg.json has depth \(1\) and chapters/01/study.html has depth \(2\). To write a link from a file \(F\) to a target \(G\), find their deepest common ancestor directory at depth \(d\), then climb out of \(F\) and walk down to \(G\):

\[ \mathrm{rel}(F \to G) \;=\; \underbrace{\bigl(\texttt{../}\bigr)^{\,d_F - d}}_{\text{climb}} \;+\; \underbrace{\text{tail}(G)}_{\text{descend}}, \]

where the exponent counts repetitions of the parent segment. The rule produces every link in this pack without a special case:

Practice

Two checks: one on the path rule, one worked answer that applies the budget.

Answer the quick check without scrolling back. The arithmetic is deliberately small: the point is the rule, not the numbers.

Quick check
A file at chapters/02/mindmap.json links to an anchor on the chapter 3 study page. Which reference is correct?
../../chapters/03/study.html#sec-loop
../03/study.html#sec-loop
chapters/03/study.html#sec-loop
study.html#sec-loop

Worked answer — is the page over budget? A draft study page has \(n = 8\) sections with word counts

\[ 420,\; 260,\; 180,\; 540,\; 300,\; 210,\; 240,\; 150, \]

and it mounts \(k = 3\) widgets. Decide whether it fits the fifteen-minute budget, and whether it is balanced.

Step 1: total and mean.

\[ W = 420 + 260 + 180 + 540 + 300 + 210 + 240 + 150 = 2300, \qquad \bar{w} = \frac{2300}{8} = 287.5. \]

Step 2: time. With \(k = 3\),

\[ T = \frac{2300}{200} + \frac{3 \times 3}{4} = 11.5 + 2.25 = 13.75 \text{ minutes}. \]

That is inside the fifteen-minute budget with about a minute to spare, so no prose has to be cut on time grounds alone.

Step 3: balance. The band is

\[ \left[\frac{\bar{w}}{3},\; 2\bar{w}\right] = [95.8,\; 575]. \]

Every section sits inside it, so the page is balanced by the rule. That does not make it good: the 540-word section is nearly four times the size of the 150-word one, and a reader feels that unevenness even when the arithmetic forgives it. The honest conclusion is that the page passes both checks and still deserves one edit, merging the two shortest sections and splitting the longest.
